System requirements

  • macOS 26 or later, on Apple silicon or Intel.
  • A microphone — built-in or external — only if you want to use voice tracking.
  • No account, no subscription, and no internet connection for normal use.

Reporting a bug

Email the address above, or open an issue on GitHub if you already have an account there. Either way, the report is much easier to act on when it includes:

  • What you did, step by step, and what you expected to happen instead.
  • Your macOS version (Apple menu › About This Mac) and your Mac model.
  • The app version, shown in PrompterGlass › About PrompterGlass.
  • A screenshot or short screen recording, if the problem is visual.

Common questions

Voice tracking will not start

The app says why underneath the Voice tracking switch:

  • “Pick a script first” — voice tracking follows the script you are reading, so it needs one selected in the Library with some text in it.
  • “Mic access denied” — open System Settings › Privacy & Security › Microphone and switch PrompterGlass on. The link in the app takes you straight there.
  • “Downloading the … speech model” — the first activation in a given language asks macOS to install its on-device speech model. This needs an internet connection once, and only once.
  • “Voice tracking unavailable for this language” — macOS has no on-device speech model for your system language yet. Fixed-speed scrolling works normally in every language.

Does my voice leave my Mac?

No. Speech is transcribed by Apple’s on-device recognition, in memory, and is never stored or transmitted. The full detail is in the privacy policy.

The overlay is hidden while I share my screen

Sharing your entire screen shares the overlay along with it. Share a single window instead, and the overlay stays on your side of the call. Some fullscreen games and certain Stage Manager arrangements can also cover the overlay.

The overlay is in the way

Drag it anywhere by its body — it reopens where you left it. Turn on Click-through to let the mouse pass straight through it while you work underneath.

Where are my scripts stored?

In the app’s own sandboxed container on your Mac. Deleting a script removes it; uninstalling the app removes everything it ever saved.
